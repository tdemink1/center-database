### The big picture
U.S. stocks split on Monday as the tech selloff resumed while the rest of the market held up. The S&P 500 fell 0.51% to 7,541.40 and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.07%, while the Dow Jones rose 0.41% and the Russell 2000 slipped 0.21%. The VIX rose 8.9% to 21.15. The day's theme was rotation out of chips and AI-linked names into financials, consumer and defensive stocks, helped by a sharp drop in oil.

### What's driving it
- **Tech and chips under pressure:** The selloff that followed last week's weak megacap results extended; the Nasdaq-100 fell 1.39% and is roughly 9% below its early-October record. SMH fell 2.7%.
- **Oil plunge:** WTI fell 7.7% to about $96 and Brent dropped to roughly $104 on reports that Washington wants to give diplomacy with Iran another try. Lower energy costs supported the Dow and cyclicals.
- **Palantir results:** Palantir reported after the close Q3 revenue of $2.31 billion (up 96%), beat estimates and raised its full-year revenue outlook; U.S. commercial revenue topped U.S. government revenue for the first time.
- **Rate-hike backdrop:** Futures price about a 76% chance of a 25 bp Fed hike in December, keeping pressure on high-valuation stocks.

### Rates & macro
The 10-year Treasury yield eased about 3 bp to 4.89% and the 30-year sits near 5.27%. ISM Manufacturing PMI for October came in at 55.5 versus roughly 54.5–54.6 expected (54.5 prior), the tenth straight month of expansion, with the prices-paid gauge easing to 75.2 from 77.9 but still elevated. The Fed funds range is 3.75%–4.00% after the Oct. 28 hold.

### Global
European stocks rose, with the CAC 40 up 0.98% and the DAX up 0.63%. Asia was mixed: the Sensex gained 2.08% and Hang Seng 1.89%, while Taiwan's TAIEX fell 3.12% and the Nikkei 2.44%. Gold rose 1.44% to about $3,955 and silver 3.05%. The dollar index was little changed near 101.8. Bitcoin fell about 2% to roughly $76,700.

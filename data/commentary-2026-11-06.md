**Weekly Wrap: week of Nov 2–Nov 6**

### The big picture
Wall Street ended a volatile week on a mixed note Friday: the S&P 500 rose 0.31%, the Nasdaq Composite 1.09% and the Russell 2000 0.89%, while the Dow slipped 0.46%. The VIX fell 9% to 25.64, still elevated after Thursday's AI-led selloff, which was the worst one-day loss for the Nasdaq, S&P 500 and Russell 2000 since April 2025. Friday's theme was a tech rebound against a backdrop of rising rate-hike expectations.

### What's driving it
- **Strong jobs data:** October payrolls rose 165,000 versus roughly 59,000–65,000 expected, and unemployment dipped to 4.1% (forecast 4.2%).
- **Chip rebound:** Semiconductors bounced after the "violent" selloff, though analysts warn volatility may persist.
- **Rate-hike pricing:** Fed funds futures now put a December hike above 77%, up from above 66% a day earlier.
- **Politics:** Midterm results gave Democrats control of both chambers, with investors weighing possible effects on data-center buildout.

### Rates & macro
The 10-year Treasury yield finished near 4.86% (+2 bp); the 2-year was about 4.52% and the 30-year about 5.26%. Fed funds sit at 3.75%–4.00% after the Oct 28 hold. Prelim Michigan sentiment (forecast 48.1, prior 46.3) was also due; I did not verify the actual reading.

### Week ahead
Next week's U.S. calendar details and consensus forecasts could not be verified, so none are listed. The next FOMC decision is Dec 9; the ECB and Bank of England meet Dec 17 and the BoJ Dec 17–18.

### Global
Europe closed lower across the board, with the DAX the weakest at -1.92% and the CAC 40 at -0.81%. In Asia, India's Sensex gained 4.82% while Korea's KOSPI fell 10.98%. WTI crude was little changed (-0.10%) and gold fell 0.94%.

### Watchlist takeaways
Sixteen of 33 names rose Friday. Leaders were AMD (+11.04%), SPCX (+8.62%) and CRWV (+6.28%); laggards were BFGIX (-4.68%), ISRG (-3.36%) and CB (-1.78%). For the week, many AI and growth names fell sharply, led by weakness in the 15–22% range in several holdings. Micron attracted debate over its valuation. No rating or price-target changes were verified.

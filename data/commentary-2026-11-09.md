### The big picture
U.S. stocks rebounded sharply after last week's selloff, which was the S&P 500's biggest weekly drop since April 2025. The S&P 500 gained 2.35% to 7,549.94, the Dow 2.15% to 52,389.75, the Nasdaq Composite 2.74% and the Russell 2000 3.15%. The VIX fell 22% to 19.92. Falling oil and a chip-stock bounce set the tone, while Nvidia was a notable exception.

### What's driving it
- **Oil slide eased inflation worries:** WTI fell 4.2% to about $87 and Brent 5.6% to about $93 after President Trump downplayed the chance of renewed strikes on Iran.
- **Chips bounce:** AMD (+13.3%) and Broadcom (+8.8%) led, recovering part of last week's rout; some analysts called the decline an overreaction.
- **Nvidia the outlier:** NVDA fell 4.3% (down about 6% at its low) on reports of a board investigation involving CEO Jensen Huang.
- **Breadth:** 29 of 33 watchlist names rose, with cyclicals such as Caterpillar (+7.2%) and GE Vernova (+7.1%) strong.

### Rates & macro
The 10-year Treasury yield was about 4.86% and the 30-year about 5.26%. Investors still worry about a December Fed hike after last Friday's stronger-than-expected jobs data; CME FedWatch put the odds near 77–80% in recent days (not re-verified after today's close). October CPI is due tomorrow at 8:30 a.m. ET.

### Global
Europe closed broadly higher: Euro Stoxx 50 +1.94%, DAX +1.73%, FTSE 100 +1.02%, FTSE MIB +2.69%. Asia was mixed: the KOSPI fell 6.78% (cause not verified), the Nikkei 3.77% and Hang Seng 1.40%, while India's Sensex rose 2.27%. Gold gained 1.8% to about $4,148; the dollar index slipped 0.6%. Bitcoin was up 0.4% near $74,900.
